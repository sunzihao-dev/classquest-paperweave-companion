PaperWeave: source-linked claim workshop

Completed teaching resource, prepared after the June classroom account. The 18 cards are authored examples, not additional student responses or a validated assessment. Their labels and rationales are AI-assisted and should be checked by the adopting teacher.

Suggested implementation

Begin with E1 (component identity), E6 (dataset scope), E7 (choice versus necessity) and E9 (comparison versus history). These eight cards form a proposed core; the remaining ten are extensions. Check the vocabulary first. Model one source-based explanation, then give pairs a different card, followed by an individual explanation on an unseen relation. Choose timings according to prior knowledge; this is not a reconstruction of the June sessions.

Keep the answer key separate. Obtain an initial answer before supplying the relevant source context. Require a location and explanation, then permit peer challenge and revision. Eventually remove the leading prompts while retaining access to sources. Fading is motivated by prior educational work, not evaluated here. Do not score students from the judgment label alone.

E1 · BERT → DPR

Relationship: component use

Supported claim: The reported DPR retriever uses two independent BERT encoders for questions and passages.

Challenge: The reported DPR retriever shares one BERT encoder parameter set between questions and passages. (refuted)

The architecture description explicitly specifies independent networks. Sharing the BERT architecture does not entail sharing one parameter set.

Repair: Restore the distinction between the question encoder and the passage encoder.

  • DPR, PDF page 3, section 3.1, Encoders. Locator anchor: “two independent BERT”. Read the surrounding paragraph.

Cards: C07, C15. AI-assisted source inspection; no independent human annotation.

E2 · BERT → BART

Relationship: author-stated conceptual relation

Supported claim: The BART paper compares its bidirectional encoder with BERT within a broader sequence-to-sequence architecture.

Challenge: BART is initialized by loading a released BERT checkpoint. (refuted)

The comparison concerns architecture. The model section separately specifies random parameter initialization, rather than BERT checkpoint initialization.

Repair: Describe the architectural comparison without asserting checkpoint reuse.

  • BART, PDF page 1, section Abstract and 1. Locator anchor: “generalizing BERT”. Read the surrounding paragraph.
  • BART, PDF page 2, section 2.1. Locator anchor: “initialise parameters”. Read the surrounding paragraph.

E3 · BERT → T5

Relationship: author-stated conceptual relation

Supported claim: T5 describes its baseline denoising objective as inspired by BERT masked language modelling and word dropout.

Challenge: T5 describes BERT as the sole inspiration for its baseline denoising objective. (refuted)

The sentence crosses a page boundary and names a second influence. Reading only its first page drops a consequential part of the statement.

Repair: Retain both named influences and restrict the claim to the baseline objective.

  • T5, PDF page 12, section 3.1.4 (continues on page 13). Locator anchor: “Inspired by BERT”. Read the surrounding paragraph.
  • T5, PDF page 13, section 3.1.4. Locator anchor: “word dropout”. Read the surrounding paragraph.

E4 · DPR → RAG

Relationship: component use

Supported claim: RAG initializes its retriever from DPR and keeps the document encoder fixed during its reported fine-tuning.

Challenge: RAG fine-tunes both the DPR question encoder and the document encoder in its reported training procedure. (refuted)

Initialization and subsequent training are different claims. The training subsection identifies the fixed document encoder.

Repair: Separate initialization from fine-tuning and preserve which encoder stays fixed.

  • RAG, PDF page 3, section 2.2. Locator anchor: “pre-trained bi-encoder”. Read the surrounding paragraph.
  • RAG, PDF page 4, section 2.4. Locator anchor: “keep the document encoder”. Read the surrounding paragraph.

Cards: C01, C06. AI-assisted source inspection; no independent human annotation.

E5 · BART → RAG

Relationship: component use

Supported claim: RAG uses a BART-large generator and fine-tunes that generator in the reported procedure.

Challenge: RAG keeps its BART generator parameters fixed throughout the reported fine-tuning. (refuted)

The generator and training subsections identify both the component and its update policy.

Repair: State that the generator is fine-tuned; do not transfer the document encoder policy to it.

E7 · T5 → FID

Relationship: experimental instantiation

Supported claim: The reported FiD experiments initialize from T5; the method description also allows a model such as BART.

Challenge: The FiD method requires T5 and excludes BART as a possible sequence-to-sequence model. (refuted)

The method explicitly gives another possible model. An experimental instantiation is not an architectural requirement.

Repair: Keep the T5 initialization claim within the scope of the reported experiments.

  • FiD, PDF page 3, section 3, Reading. Locator anchor: “such as T5 or”. Read the surrounding paragraph.
  • FiD, PDF page 4, section 4, Technical details. Locator anchor: “initialize our models”. Read the surrounding paragraph.

E8 · RAG → FID

Relationship: author-stated conceptual relation

Supported claim: The FiD paper compares its handling of retrieved passages with RAG and describes joint processing in the decoder.

Challenge: The comparison establishes that FiD loads a previously trained RAG system as a component. (not established)

The comparison and method description do not establish the asserted component inheritance. Withhold that claim; a comparison is not a lineage record.

Repair: Retain the processing comparison. To assert reuse, obtain explicit implementation or initialization evidence.

  • FiD, PDF page 3, section 3, Reading. Locator anchor: “jointly in the decoder”. Read the surrounding paragraph.

E9 · T5 → BART

Relationship: curator comparison

Supported claim: T5 and BART can be compared as encoder-decoder approaches with denoising pre-training.

Challenge: Their shared encoder-decoder and denoising properties establish that T5 historically influenced BART. (not established)

Shared properties support an analytical comparison. They do not supply evidence of the direction or existence of historical influence.

Repair: Label the relation as a curator comparison; request a historical or author-attribution source before claiming influence.
